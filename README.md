# 🏔️ Mountaineering Fatality Analysis

An end-to-end data analysis and machine learning project exploring **fatalities across major Himalayan peaks**, with a deep dive into Mount Everest.

This project combines:
- 📊 Statistical analysis  
- 🌦️ Weather data exploration  
- 🤖 Machine learning modeling  
- 🗺️ Interactive geospatial visualization  

👉 **The goal:** understand *why climbers die* — and whether we can predict fatal outcomes.

## 📌 Key Insights

### 🌍 Nationality Trends

> **Nepalese climbers account for the highest number of fatalities across all peaks.**

This reflects their critical role in expeditions, taking on disproportionate risk as the commercialization of mountaineering becomes increasingly popular.

![Deaths by Nationality](https://github.com/kerwehner/MountaineeringAnalysis/wiki/images/bynationality15.png)

---

### 📈 Commercialization & Rising Fatalities

> **Fatalities have increased alongside the commercialization of high-altitude climbing.**

More climbers + guided expeditions = more exposure to risk.

![Deaths Over Time](https://github.com/kerwehner/MountaineeringAnalysis/wiki/images/overtime.png)

---

### 🧗 Everest Death Zone Analysis

> **Deaths cluster heavily above 8,000 meters — the "Death Zone" — where oxygen is critically low and exhaustion peaks.**

![Everest Death Profile](https://github.com/kerwehner/MountaineeringAnalysis/wiki/images/everest_death_vertical_profile.png)

## 🏔️ Machine Learning

### Key Findings

- 📈 **Fatalities are increasing over time**
  - ~0.22 additional deaths per year  

- 💰 **Commercialization is a major driver**
  - Deaths have nearly doubled in the modern era  

- 🤖 **Prediction remains difficult**
  - Outcomes are complex and situational  

- 🏔️ **Risk trends vary by mountain**
  - Increasing: Everest, Makalu  
  - Decreasing: Annapurna, Manaslu  

- 🧍 **Human factors outperform weather variables**

- 🏔️ **Altitude is highly predictive**

- 🌦️ **Weather adds limited predictive value**  

---

## 🧠 Final Conclusion

> Mountaineering fatalities are driven by the interaction of:

- 🏔️ Altitude  
- 🧍 Human decision-making  
- 🌦️ Environmental conditions  

**No single factor explains the fatalities in mountaineering, however human decision-making consistently stands out as the dominant influence.**
